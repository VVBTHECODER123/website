Dr. Rakesh Batabyal

Historian of Modern and Contemporary Times

Dr. Rakesh Batabyal stands as an eminent Indian historian, media scholar, and profoundly reflective public intellectual who seamlessly bridges the past with the complexities of contemporary media landscapes. Born on December 15, 1966, his scholarly contributions span modern history, media structures, institutional evolution, and the political historiography of South Asia.

Driven by a deep humanistic commitment, his work unpacks the friction between technological advancement and human well-being. Renowned for his meticulous institutional insights and sharp cultural commentary, he continually challenges conventional narratives surrounding nationalism, memory, and political discourse.

As an Associate Professor at the Centre for Media Studies, School of Social Sciences, Jawaharlal Nehru University (JNU), New Delhi, he remains a vital voice in fostering critical thinking—inspiring scholars to examine the ethical dimensions of history and modern media systems.

Selected Articles & Journal Papers

Book Chapter | 2011

Countering Hegemony: The Polish Solidarity Movement in the Memory of Indians

Published by the Institute of National Remembrance, Warsaw. Examines Indian perspectives on the Polish Solidarity movement, evaluating Cold War diplomatic memory and cross-cultural historical alignment.

Journal Article | 2013

The Imperial Embers and the Invocation to Cold War: Colombo Conference 1950

Published in *Proceedings of the Indian History Congress*. Analyzes post-colonial Asian foreign policy shifts, detailing how British imperial decline shaped early Asian diplomatic initiatives.

Historiographical Review | 2014

Published in *Studies in History* (Sage). Dissects the long-term socio-political aftermath, border demographics, and ongoing administrative ripples of Bengal’s partition.

Journal Article | 2021

Published in *Aparajito Satyajit*, Kalyani University. Investigates Satyajit Ray’s cinematic lens and how visual narratives articulate themes of human agency and modern political morality.
